# Phase 9 — Rules and calculation

An overtime rule belongs to one legal entity and one calendar day type: working day, rest day, or national holiday. Rules are approved, effective-dated, and non-overlapping per day type. The configuration records calculation method classification, employment eligibility, minimum duration, rounding increment and mode, maximum duration, ordered rate segments, and optional meal and transport thresholds and IDR amounts. Emergency is a request classification, not a special night-shift category; the effective calendar still selects the day rule. No government coefficient, company multiplier, maximum, allowance, or eligibility condition is seeded as a production policy.

All duration arithmetic uses integer minutes. Segment multipliers use integer hundredths: `150` represents a factor of 1.50 without floating-point arithmetic. Segments are ordered by cumulative minute cap and the last cap is null so every payable duration has an explicit rule. The calculation trace records candidate minutes, rounding mode, payable minutes, each segment's consumed minutes, multiplier hundredths, and weighted minute hundredths. A SHA-256 checksum protects the exact rule snapshot used by the immutable calculation.

Actual minutes come from current normalized attendance. On a working day they begin at the later of scheduled end and check-in, then end at check-out. On a rest day or national holiday they span check-in to check-out. Candidate payable minutes are capped by actual, manager-approved, and policy maximum durations. Below-minimum results become zero; otherwise configured rounding applies and final output remains capped by approval and policy maximum. Meal and transport eligibility derives only from payable minutes and configured thresholds. Phase 9 produces eligible units and allowance inputs; it deliberately does not calculate overtime wages, salary, tax, BPJS, or net pay. HR/Legal must validate any regulation-based rule against current official sources before business activation.
