# Gross-to-net engine and snapshots

The Phase 10 engine uses fixed-scale decimal strings and integer operations, never PHP floating-point arithmetic. Values are normalized to four decimals; multiplication, ratios, comparisons, addition, subtraction, and component rounding are explicit. Recurring fixed-monthly lines are prorated by segment days over period days. Daily-rate lines multiply by eligible segment days. Component economic type determines whether an amount accumulates into gross income, employee deduction, or employer cost. Net is gross minus employee deductions.

Approved unpaid leave is consumed only when an effective employee salary line uses the `unpaid_leave_daily` calculation type. Without that configured policy, the run emits a warning and does not invent a deduction. Payroll-eligible Phase 9 overtime calculations may contribute configured meal and transport allowance components. Eligible overtime minutes deliberately produce a Phase 11 warning because the wage rate and statutory formula are not implemented here. PPh 21 and BPJS totals remain exactly zero with an explicit statutory-pending warning; zero is not presented as a legal result.

Each calculation creates an employee snapshot containing employment and organization labels, service dates, payable and period days, approved salary-version checksums and logical segments, encrypted bank evidence limited to payment-relevant fields, and detailed calculation items. Every item records quantity, rate, base, unrounded value, rounded amount, currency, source type and reference, sequence, and trace metadata. A SHA-256 snapshot checksum covers the employee, bank, salary, item, and period evidence. Run totals aggregate those immutable employee results.

Snapshots cannot be recalculated in place. If bank verification, attendance, salary, membership, or another source is corrected, Payroll creates a new run version. The old result remains immutable for audit comparison. The foundation ends at `validated`; later phases must define independent review, final approval, locking, reopening through superseding evidence, payment file controls, accounting reconciliation, payslip publication, and statutory calculations.
